What is it used for?

Useful for understanding a foreign speedometer, following a sports match, calculating travel time, or reading weather data (knots).

Concrete examples

  • A car on the highway drives at 130 km/h, or about 80 mph.
  • A high-speed train runs at 320 km/h, or about 200 mph.
  • An airliner flies at 900 km/h, or about Mach 0.74.
  • A knot = 1 nautical mile per hour = 1.852 km/h.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Don't confuse km/h and mph: 100 mph ≠ 100 km/h (100 mph = 161 km/h).
  • The knot is a maritime and aeronautical unit, not terrestrial.
  • Mach depends on altitude and temperature: it's not a fixed speed.

FAQ

How many km/s in 200 km/h?

How many km/h in 1 mph?
1 mph = 1.60934 km/h exactly. Conversely, 1 km/h = 0.621371 mph. For quick math: multiply mph by 1.6.

Why are knots used at sea and in the air?
The knot is tied to the nautical mile, which corresponds to 1 minute of latitude arc. It simplifies position calculations on a nautical chart.

What is Mach?
Mach is the ratio between an object's speed and the speed of sound in the medium. Mach 1 = speed of sound ≈ 340 m/s in air at sea level. Mach depends on altitude and temperature.

Practical tips

  • To convert mph to km/h, multiply by 1.6.
  • To convert km/h to mph, divide by 1.6.
  • A knot ≈ 1.852 km/h ≈ 0.514 m/s.
