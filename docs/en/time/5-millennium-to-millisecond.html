What is it used for?

Useful in programming (milliseconds), project management (days, weeks, quarters), history (centuries, millennia), or understanding contract durations.

Concrete examples

  • An eye blink lasts about 300 milliseconds.
  • A year is 365.25 days (hence leap years).
  • A French presidential term lasts 5 years.
  • A century = 100 years, a millennium = 1000 years.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • A month doesn't have a fixed length: 28, 29, 30, or 31 days.
  • A year isn't exactly 365 days, but 365.2422 days (hence the leap calendar).
  • A quarter (3 months) also has no fixed day count.

How many seconds in a day?
1 day = 24 × 60 × 60 = 86,400 seconds. A key value for any duration calculation.

Why is a year 365.25 days?
Earth orbits the Sun in 365.2422 days. To compensate, we add a day every 4 years: February 29.

What's the difference between a lustrum and a quinquennium?
A lustrum lasts 5 years (Roman period), a quinquennium too (5-year term, notably in French politics). Both equal 5 years.

Practical tips

  • To convert seconds to days: divide by 86400.
