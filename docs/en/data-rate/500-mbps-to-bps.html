What is it used for?

Useful for choosing an internet plan, understanding why a download is slow, or comparing fiber/ADSL/4G/5G offers.

Concrete examples

  • An ADSL connection averages 8 Mbit/s.
  • Fiber offers between 100 Mbit/s and 1 Gbit/s.
  • 5G can reach 1 Gbit/s under good conditions.
  • A 5 GB HD movie downloads in 6 minutes at 100 Mbit/s.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Don't confuse Mbit/s (megabits per second) and MB/s (megabytes per second). 1 MB/s = 8 Mbit/s.
  • A '100 meg' plan is in Mbit/s, not MB/s. Real speed will be ~12.5 MB/s.
  • Advertised speed is a theoretical maximum, rarely achieved in practice.

FAQ

How many bit/s in 500 Mbit/s?
500 Mbit/s equals 500000000 bit/s.

How to convert Mbit/s to bit/s?
Use the calculator above.

What's the difference between Mbit/s and MB/s?
Mbit/s = megabits per second (network rate unit). MB/s = megabytes per second (file transfer unit). 1 MB/s = 8 Mbit/s. ISPs use Mbit/s to look faster.

How long to download a movie on fiber?
A 5 GB HD movie downloads in about 6 minutes at 100 Mbit/s, and in 40 seconds at 1 Gbit/s (high-end fiber).

What speed for 4K streaming?
4K needs between 25 and 50 Mbit/s depending on the platform (Netflix, YouTube). Standard HD needs 5 Mbit/s. Fiber is recommended for multiple simultaneous users.

Practical tips

  • To estimate download time: size (MB) ÷ speed (MB/s) = seconds.
  • To go from Mbit/s to MB/s, divide by 8.
  • Fiber is the only way to reliably reach 1 Gbit/s.
