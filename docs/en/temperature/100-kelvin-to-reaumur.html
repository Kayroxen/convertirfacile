What is it used for?

Useful for weather, cooking, air conditioning, body temperature, or understanding recipes and scientific data.

Concrete examples

  • Water freezes at 0 °C (32 °F) and boils at 100 °C (212 °F).
  • Normal body temperature is 37 °C (98.6 °F).
  • Absolute zero is at -273.15 °C (0 Kelvin).
  • A hot summer day is 30 °C (86 °F).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Kelvin has no degree (°) symbol: say '300 kelvins', not '300 degrees Kelvin'.
  • Watch for negative signs: -40 °C = -40 °F (unique equality point).
  • Rankine and Réaumur are barely used anymore, but still exist in some fields.

What's the formula to convert Celsius to Fahrenheit?
°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32. Example: 20 °C = (20 × 9/5) + 32 = 36 + 32 = 68 °F. The reverse formula: °C = (°F - 32) × 5/9.

What is absolute zero?
Absolute zero is the lowest possible temperature: -273.15 °C or 0 Kelvin. At this temperature, molecules have no thermal energy left. It's a theoretical concept.

Why does the US use Fahrenheit?
Fahrenheit was invented by Daniel Gabriel Fahrenheit in 1724. The US adopted it before the metric system spread and never abandoned it. A few other countries use it too (Bahamas, Belize, Cayman Islands).

Practical tips

  • To roughly convert °C to °F: × 2 + 30.
  • To roughly convert °F to °C: (- 30) ÷ 2.
  • -40 °C = -40 °F: an easy reference to remember.
