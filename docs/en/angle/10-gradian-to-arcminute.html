What is it used for?

Useful for DIY (cuts), navigation, astronomy, surveying, or understanding radians in mathematics.

Concrete examples

  • A right angle is 90° or π/2 radians.
  • A full turn is 360° or 2π radians.
  • Latitude and longitude are expressed in degrees.
  • A 45° angle corresponds to a 100% slope.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Don't confuse degrees (°) and radians (rad): 180° = π rad.
  • A gradian (400 for a full turn) is neither a degree nor a radian.
  • Arcminutes and arcseconds aren't minutes of time.

What's the difference between degrees and radians?
The degree divides a circle into 360 parts. The radian measures the angle by arc length on the unit circle. A full turn = 360° = 2π radians. Radians are used in math and physics.

What is the gradian used for?
The gradian divides a full turn into 400 parts. It's used in surveying and the military. 100 gradians = 90°. It's also called a 'centesimal degree'.

How to convert degrees to radians?
Multiply by π and divide by 180. Example: 45° = 45 × π/180 = π/4 ≈ 0.7854 rad. Remember: 180° = π rad, 90° = π/2, 60° = π/3, 45° = π/4.

Practical tips

  • To convert degrees to radians: multiply by π/180.
  • To convert radians to degrees: multiply by 180/π.
  • A right angle = 90° = π/2 rad ≈ 1.5708 rad.
